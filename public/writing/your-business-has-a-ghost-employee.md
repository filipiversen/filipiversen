# Your Business Has a Ghost Employee

By Filip Iversen

[Canonical page](https://www.filipiversen.com/writing/your-business-has-a-ghost-employee)

Find the manual work scattered across your team and work out which parts are worth automating.

Published: 2026-05-06

Pipeline updates copied between tools. Follow-ups tracked in a notebook.
The report someone rebuilds from scratch every month. Each task looks
small enough to live with, so nobody adds up the time.

Together, they can amount to a substantial part of someone’s job. I think
of that scattered manual work as a ghost employee: work you’re paying for
without seeing it on the org chart.

## Why it stays invisible

The work is distributed. It’s five minutes here, a recurring twenty there,
spread across people who each assume it’s part of the job. There’s no line
item called “moving data by hand,” so nobody has a clear reason to question it.

## Find it, name it, price it

Walk through the operation and write down the recurring manual processes.
For each one, estimate the time spent and the full hourly cost of the
people doing it, including benefits and overhead:

```text
hours per week × loaded hourly rate × 52 = annual cost
```

Use the number of active weeks if the work is seasonal. The estimate
doesn’t have to be precise to help you compare tasks, but it should come
from the people doing the work.

Time saved also doesn’t automatically become money saved. Often the
benefit is giving someone more time for work you already need them to do.
Be clear about which outcome you expect.

## Be willing to leave it alone

Some processes are too rare, too dependent on judgment, or too cheap to
justify automating. Compare the cost of the manual work with the effort
to build, review, run, and maintain the automation.

Aim for a short ranked list with estimated effort and payback for each
item. “Leave it alone” is a useful result for a process that already costs
less than its proposed replacement.
